# 0005: Cloud-hosted video replay demo

Status: accepted, 2026-10-03. Supersedes the on-premises worker and the rule that frames stay on the camera network in [0002](0002-live-state-pipeline-and-liff-app.md), and all of [0003](0003-team-owned-camera-network.md).

The final deliverable is a demo, not an installed camera: the worker replays a recorded video of one parking area from one fixed viewpoint at its real pace, detects motorcycles and fits candidate space on the newest frame, and the LIFF app shows the resulting state on the 3D lot model in step with the video. The video itself plays separately on the presenter's screen; the LIFF app shows only the 3D state and the video time of the analysed frame, never footage, so the rule against raw footage in the browser stands. The demo video is a phone recording of the Sripatum parking area from a fixed viewpoint, made once flooding allows and under the #16 policy; stand-in data ([0004](0004-stand-in-data-is-not-accuracy-evidence.md)) supports development but does not replace it as the demo. Every process (worker, API, Postgres, LIFF) runs on Coolify, and labelling, training, and experiments run on shared cloud services, so the team works from one environment and no member's machine carries inference. Videos and weights stay out of Git and the browser. Published states keep the `RECORDED_REPLAY` or `SIMULATED_REPLAY` source kind, and the freshness, calibration, and uncertainty rules of 0002 apply unchanged, so a live camera can be added later as another source without changing the contract. Rejected: the team-owned camera network and on-premises worker of 0003, because permission, mounting, power, and the 4G uplink sat on the critical path for a capability the final presentation does not need. Accepted cost: the project claims nothing about live operation, camera uptime, or latency in the real lot, and real footage stored in the cloud must be allowed by the #16 footage policy, which now covers cloud storage and access.
