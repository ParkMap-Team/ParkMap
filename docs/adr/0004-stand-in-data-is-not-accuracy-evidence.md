# 0004: Stand-in data is not accuracy evidence

Status: accepted, 2026-10-03.

While flooding keeps the Sripatum lot empty, development and the M1 demo run on stand-in data: public motorcycle datasets, simulated scenes rendered from the lot model, and AI-generated video. The single real-angle photo is a sanity check and calibration demo only, not a test set. Stand-in metrics are always reported with their source and never as accuracy in the real lot; the #5 release gates and the #7 false-green measurement still require real-angle footage collected under the #16 policy. A simulated scene, rendered or AI-generated, replayed through the worker publishes under a new source kind `SIMULATED_REPLAY`, which the LIFF app announces like the other non-live sources; `RECORDED_REPLAY` stays reserved for real recordings. Rendered scenes serve mainly as exact-ground-truth tests of homography, fitting, capacity, and false-green logic; AI-generated video has no ground truth and only demonstrates the pipeline; detector training prefers real public images, because the sim-to-real gap is large for dense, occluded motorcycles. Rejected: treating public or simulated metrics as the project's accuracy evidence, because they say nothing about the real camera angle, lighting, or parking density, and a false green in the real lot is the costliest failure. Accepted cost: M1 shows no real-angle accuracy, and if the lot is still inaccessible on 26 Oct the team renegotiates M3 scope with the advisor rather than substituting stand-in numbers.
